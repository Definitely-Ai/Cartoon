# The Harrington Vision

Purpose: the founder's actual vision for The Swinging Door, established by the four
reference plates in `canon/vision/` (the prints signed **Harrington**) and by his
review notes in `feedback/model-review/`. **This document sits at the top of the
authority ladder: wherever any older bible, sheet, or prompt disagrees with it,
this document and the plates win.** The plates are the pictures; this page is the
law read off them.

## The series in one breath

Two regulars — a flamingo and a golden retriever — read the news the way America
actually reads it: from a barstool. The Swinging Door is their bar, a
nineteenth-hole room where the TV chyron, the chalkboard specials, and the
banners on the wall are all in on the joke, and the caption underneath is one
dry, attributed line. The comedy is Washington-adjacent household economics:
debt ceilings, tariffs, retirement planning, airport security, the price of a
martini by fare class. Nobody shouts. The room does half the talking.

## The cast

### Drew — the flamingo

**Definitive study: `canon/vision/drew-reference.jpg`** — the founder's words:
*"drew must look like this."*

- A **white-plumed flamingo**, plump and soft-bodied, drawn feather by feather.
- **Neck**: a true flamingo neck — long, and curved in the deep, elegant
  question-mark sweep of the plates. The founder's note: *"neck curve …
  needs to look more like a flamingo."* Never a straight or goose-stiff neck.
- **Head and beak**: compact head; the heavy downturned flamingo beak,
  pale with a bold black tip.
- **Eyes**: **heavy-lidded and deadpan** — visible white, distinct dark iris
  and pupil, a drooping upper lid and drawn brow that read as dry unimpressed
  intelligence. Human-readable, never a bird dot (the founder asked for this
  explicitly, and the close-up plate delivers exactly it).
- **Wardrobe**: a **knitted sweater vest** (fine cable or rib texture) over a
  pale collared shirt, with his small solid-black **bow tie** always at the
  throat — and **trousers** (founder's note: "he needs to have pants").
  At golf he adds a **visor**; themed additions stay in this key: knitwear,
  club-house, never a costume.
- **Hands**: feathered **four-fingered hands with an opposed thumb** and a
  soft fluffy cuff at the wrist — they hold a martini stem, a pen, a checklist
  with believable human grips, and drape at rest like a gloved human hand;
  long scalloped tiers of wing feathers hang beneath each forearm (founder's
  note: "his hands need to be changed … more human").
- **Collar**: a crisp starched white collar band circles the neck base under
  the bow tie.
- **Drink**: the martini, conical stemmed glass, olives on a pick, on a napkin
  or coaster — always within reach at the bar; lifted by the stem in a dainty,
  pinky-elegant grip.
- **Staging**: frame-left by default, the neck arched to hang his face where
  the gag needs it, eye sliding sideways at Mango. He is the brightest object
  in the panel — the paper's white is reserved for him. A monocle is an
  approved occasional accessory.
- **Read**: the arch observer. He names the mechanism of the absurdity in one
  clean sentence.

### Mango — the golden retriever

**Definitive study: `canon/vision/mango-reference.jpg`.**

- **Unmistakably a golden retriever, drawn like one** (founder's note: "not
  just a dog but a golden retriever"): layered, feathered fur in fine strokes,
  drop ears with long fringe, the broad soft-eyed retriever face with freckled
  muzzle — and **true black dog lips**, the dark lip-line along the muzzle under
  his black nose (the founder loves this: "it's like a dog"). Middle-aged,
  solid, rounded shoulders, modest belly. **No tail.**
- **Neck-beard trimmed**: some chest ruff, but kept modest (founder's note:
  "his neck beard is too large").
- **Wardrobe, two registers** (both canon, chosen by the scene):
  - **Bar default — the polo**: a pale collared polo shirt with the **small
    USA flag pin on the left chest** of the shirt, a **wristwatch**, and a
    **belt** over trousers. This is the plates' most common Mango.
  - **Dressed up — the jacket**: a rumpled suit jacket over a collared shirt,
    flag pin on the **left lapel**, for panels with a formal flavor.
  - At golf: white polo and a **ball cap**; caps are natural on Mango.
- **Accessories**: wristwatch on the left wrist, always; at golf a white
  glove on the left paw and his bag with the towel lettered FORE! NOT SO FAST.
- **Drink**: the old fashioned — short rocks glass, one large cube, a dark
  cherry — at frame-right, with the shared nut bowl centered between his glass
  and Drew's.
- **Read**: the worried, earnest everyman against Drew's deadpan — raised
  inner brows, hangdog patience. He pays the bill; his lines are wallet-height
  truths.

### Abby — the westie who owns the bar

**Definitive face study: `canon/vision/abby-reference.jpg`** — the founder
likes this Abby, with two corrections: the bust drawn **modest** ("too big"
was the fault), and **he loves the collar** — the studded leather collar with
its small teardrop gem pendant is her signature neckwear now. Her role is
unchanged — **owner and bartender of The Swinging Door** — drawn in the
plates' technique:

- **Fine, dense fur detail in the face** — short-muzzled West Highland
  terrier, round black nose, bright expressive eyes; the fur drawn in fine
  individual strokes, **never coarse or wolfish** (founder's note: "not so she
  looks like a werewolf").
- **Figure**: trim and natural — hips, knees, and legs drawn slim (founder's
  note: "legs, knees, hips are slightly wide" was a fault).
- **True fluffy westie face**: round dark button eyes, black nose, small
  pricked ears — cute and bright, drawn soft.
- **The collar**: a studded leather collar with a small teardrop gem pendant
  at its center — her signature, replacing the pearl strand; a pearl
  bracelet stays at her wrist.
- **Modest bust, always** — never exaggerated.
- Work wardrobe: fitted blouse with rolled sleeves, short dark skirt, folded
  towel on the left shoulder, black heels. **No tail.**
- Behind her counter is her territory; patrons stay on the room side.

## The Swinging Door — the room

A classic dark-wood barroom with a **nineteenth-hole soul** — the bar of a
golf-club neighborhood in a company town that happens to be Washington:

- Framed script signs reading *"The Swinging Door"* on the walls (and the
  window sign reversed from inside); leather club chairs and stools with
  brass studs; framed prints of airplanes, landmarks, golfers.
- The **back bar** carries rows of bottles whose labels are golf puns:
  OLD FORESTER, 10TH HOLE RYE, BIRDIE BOURBON, EAGLE EYE RYE, PAR-TEE SCOTCH,
  19TH HOLE RYE, DIVOT DRIVE GIN, HAZARD SPICED WHISKY — that register.
- A **large flatscreen TV over the back bar** playing the news with full
  broadcast grammar — **DCN** network bug + **LIVE** tag, bold-caps headline
  chyron on a lower-third band, a smaller ticker sentence beneath, a time
  stamp (*5:17 ET*) — the on-screen picture drawn in the same engraved style
  (*NATIONAL MALL RENOVATION TROUBLES* over workers skimming the reflecting
  pool; *RETIREMENT PLANNING — YOUR FUTURE SELF IS WATCHING*). Definitive
  study: `canon/vision/tv-reference.jpg` — the founder's words: "tv should
  be like this."
- A **chalkboard** carrying menu-shaped jokes: *FIRST CLASS MARTINI $18 ·
  PREMIUM ECONOMY MARTINI $17 · BASIC ECONOMY MARTINI $17*; *TODAY'S SPECIAL:
  IMPORTED BEER $8 · PATRIOTIC IMPORTED BEER $12*; *Special: NANOBUBBLES
  $17.76*; *WEEKDAY DRINK SPECIAL! EARLY BIRD OLD FASHIONED $7*.
- Occasional event dressing: banners and balloons (*16th ANNUAL DEBT CEILING
  WEEK — WE ALMOST DID IT! SAME TIME NEXT YEAR!*), commemorative-T-shirt
  signs, small framed house rules (*NO CHASING YOUR BALLS IN HERE*).
- The shared bowl of bar nuts centered between the two regulars' drinks —
  a running prop; coasters and napkins under every glass; a globe when the
  joke needs one. The room is
  **furnished with jokes** — props earn their place by being able to carry one.
- Away games exist and keep the same eye: the airport security line plate is
  canon (*ALL PASSENGERS ALL THE TIME, NO EXCEPTIONS (ESPECIALLY YOU)*;
  *PREPARE TO BE HUMBLED*; the threat-level dial *ELEVATED — TOMORROW'S
  PROBABLY TOO*). Golf course, security line, anywhere Americana — the signage
  comes along.

## The rendering style

The plates are drawn like **antique steel engravings**:

- Fine pen crosshatching and stippling everywhere; every surface textured —
  wood grain, knit stitches, fur laid in individual strokes, glass reflections.
- **Full tonal range**: deep rich blacks (chalkboards, jackets, shadows)
  through silvery middle greys to bright paper highlights. Density is the
  style: the panel is FULL, corner to corner, and rewards a second look.
  (The old "generous white space / three values" rule is retired.)
- Confident contour lines over the hatch; no flat fills, no cel shading, no
  photographic rendering, no color.
- Composition: eye-level or gently raised, characters large in frame,
  the gag surfaces (board, TV, banner) composed to be read.
- Hand-lettered signage in period style — chalk serifs and flourishes on the
  boards, block capitals on notices.
- A small artist signature tucked in an edge is in the plates' character;
  the strip's own panels may carry the house signature or none.

## Text — the two-layer system

**Layer one — inside the panel.** In-image lettering is a *feature*: menu
boards, chyrons, banners, labels, and signs carry secondary gags. Keep each
piece short, hand-lettered, and legible; the panel's funniest sentence is often
on the wall. (The founder's earlier "no lettering" rule is retired; balloons
and floating caption text inside the art remain banned — text lives ON
things.)

**Layer two — beneath the panel.** The caption is typeset under the art in
*italic serif*, as **attributed dialogue**:

> *Drew: "The security line is our most successful national team-building
> exercise."*
>
> *Mango: "I've reached the age where I don't want luxury. I just want knee
> room."*

One speaker, one line (two-line quotes wrap centered). The speaker's name
before the colon; the quote in curly quotes. This replaces the unattributed
caption format everywhere the house typesets dialogue.

## The humor register — the seven canonical gags

The plates ARE the comedy bible's examples:

1. *Drew: "The security line is our most successful national team-building exercise."* — institutional absurdity, affectionate.
2. *Mango: "I've reached the age where I don't want luxury. I just want knee room."* — aging and airline economics at wallet height.
3. *Drew: "It's comforting to see our emergencies becoming traditions."* — the debt ceiling as an annual holiday.
4. *Drew: "Retirement planning is just estimating how expensive your hobbies are going to become."* — personal finance, dry.
5. *Drew: "The republic remains blue in concept, green in operations."* — civic decay observed like weather.
6. *Mango: "I don't mind paying more. I just like to know which country I'm punishing."* — tariffs, from the barstool.
7. *Drew: "Golf is the only sport where everyone complains about the speed and then takes a practice swing."* / *"Globalization was easier when nobody knew where anything came from."* — the clubhouse worldview.

The target is systems, never people by name; the tone is rueful, fond, and
unhurried. The room's signage escalates the same joke the caption lands.

## What this supersedes

- The three-value sparse ink-wash style → replaced by full-tone engraving density.
- "No lettering anywhere in the image" → reversed; signage is a gag layer.
- Unattributed captions → attributed italic dialogue beneath the panel.
- Drew bare-feathered / bare-legged with only a bow tie → sweater vest, shirt,
  trousers; hand-like feathered hands; bible eyes on a true flamingo neck.
- Mango's lapel-only pin → chest placement on the polo (lapel when jacketed).
- The old model sheets and the v1 trained model remain history, not authority.
