Picture 1 is an interior drawn as an antique steel engraving: fine pen crosshatching and stippling, full tonal range, black and white only.
Redraw Picture 1 as EXACTLY the same room from the same camera, and make ONLY this change: REDRAW THE WALL PANELLING SO THAT IT IS REAL JOINERY.
At present every part of that wall — the panels, the rails, the stiles and the whole dado — is covered in fine, closely spaced vertical lines. It reads as corrugated boarding or a grille, not as walnut, and no joiner ever built it. Draw it instead the way panelling is actually made:
1. THE GRAIN FOLLOWS EACH MEMBER. A stile is an upright board and its grain runs up it. A RAIL IS A HORIZONTAL BOARD AND ITS GRAIN RUNS ALONG IT, HORIZONTALLY. Nowhere on this wall does grain run across a member instead of along it.
2. A PANEL FIELD IS ONE BROAD FLAT BOARD, never a bundle of stripes: its figure is a FEW soft, wide, wandering grain lines with generous smooth space between them, the quiet figure of a good walnut board. There is NO fine parallel striping, NO reeding, NO fluting and NO beading anywhere on this wall.
3. ONE MODULE, REPEATED. The wall divides into BAYS OF EQUAL WIDTH. Each bay carries one tall panel, and the stile between every pair of bays is the same width as every other stile. No bay is noticeably wider or narrower than its neighbours.
4. EVERY PANEL HAS THE SAME SURROUND: one moulded profile, the same width on all four sides, mitred neatly at the corners, and every panel's field is raised the same amount.
5. THE FRAME IS CLOSED. A horizontal TOP RAIL runs beneath the cornice above the panels, and a horizontal BOTTOM RAIL runs beneath them. Both are plainly horizontal members with horizontal grain.
6. THE DADO. Below the tall panels a moulded DADO RAIL caps a lower run of SHORT PANELS built in the same module and the same joinery as the tall ones above — NOT a band of reeding, NOT a run of vertical lines. A plain moulded skirting finishes it at the bottom.
Everything else in the picture stays exactly as it is: the window with its frame, reveal, sill and blank bright glass; the marble counter and its moulded lip and the panelling below it; the cornice where it sits; the camera; the light from the left; the drawing style; and the emptiness of the room.
The room stays completely empty: no shelves, no bottles, no television, no chalkboard, no sconces, no lamps, no furniture, no people, no animals, and not one letter or number anywhere.
