## Canonical Summary (non-normative)

This summary is for human readers; the master prompt's ABBY paragraph is the paste text.

Abby is an adult female anthropomorphic West Highland White Terrier and the owner-bartender of The Swinging Door. She has a compact Westie head with upright triangular ears, a short softly squared canine muzzle, a solid black canine nose, and detailed layered white facial fur. Her expressive human-style eyes are naturally integrated into the canine skull: visible white sclera, medium-gray irises with radial texture, round black pupils, small controlled catchlights, clear lids, refined lashes, and expressive brow-fur. Her upright adult body has a feminine hourglass silhouette with a fuller bust, narrow waist, slim hips, smooth shapely legs of short white fur, and a natural thigh gap; her hands are five-digit and anthropomorphic. She has absolutely no tail. Her locked work outfit is a fitted light collared blouse with neatly rolled sleeves and only the top button open over a modest scalloped lace inset, a very short dark fitted bartender skirt/apron with a large centered back bow, a folded bar towel on her left shoulder, one delicate bracelet, black closed-toe mid-height heels, and a close single strand of small round pearls with one centered oval faceted gemstone in a dark metal bezel. She is intelligent, quick-witted, warm, poised, competent, and unmistakably in charge of the room; her default expression is a welcoming, intelligent smile unless the scene names another approved expression.

## Character Read

Abby is **the house**: owner, bartender, memory keeper, and quiet referee. Other characters bring the argument; Abby recognizes the pattern. She is intelligent, quick-witted, warm, poised, competent, and authoritative — she reacts while completing a task rather than dropping everything to perform, and her wit is one economical gesture, never mugging. She may be dry, skeptical, firm, or gently condescending, but never cruel, ditzy, submissive, sloppy, intoxicated, or incompetent. She appears only when the gag needs her — about one cartoon in ten.

## Identity Constants

These remain true in every scene, outfit, pose, crop, and camera angle:

- Adult female Westie — not a puppy, child, mascot, generic dog, or human woman with animal ears.
- Anthropomorphic and fully upright, with human-readable posture, hands, acting, and work mechanics.
- Compact Westie head, upright triangular ears, short softly squared canine muzzle, black canine nose.
- Layered directional white facial fur; the face is textured, never smooth plastic or a uniform halo.
- Medium-sized, living human-style eyes integrated into the canine face.
- Feminine adult hourglass build: fuller bust, narrow waist, slim hips, long clean leg line.
- Smooth shapely legs of short white fur — reading as a clean paper-white contour, not skin — with subtle knee and calf definition and a natural thigh gap.
- Five-digit anthropomorphic hands with one readable thumb and four fingers.
- Feet remain inside black closed-toe mid-height heels in the work outfit.
- Absolutely no tail, tail tuft, tail opening, tail bulge, or tail-shaped garment fold.
- Close pearl strand with one centered oval gemstone; never a plain choker.
- One folded bar towel, on her left shoulder by default; never two towels.
- Default expression: a welcoming, intelligent smile unless the scene names another approved expression.
- Intelligent, witty, warm, poised, competent, and authoritative emotional baseline.
- Strict black-and-white hand-drawn ink wash: exactly three values (paper white, one mid-gray wash, solid black ink); never color.

## Proportion and Scale Anchors (working)

Working anchors derived from the sheets — the locked master outranks the numbers:

## Wardrobe Policy

Her work outfit is the default; a scene may change garments only when [SCENE] explicitly requires it; her body, her pearl-and-gem collar, and the no-tail rule never change. Scene-specific garments never carry into another scene by default.

## Blocking — One Rule

Behind the bar is the service side: Abby's territory, no one else's. When she is behind the bar the counter correctly hides her from the upper hip down — that is the right drawing, never a fault to fix. She leaves that position **only for an explicit owner action named in the scene**: opening or closing, greeting at the swinging doors, checking the room, carrying stock, or solving a practical problem. She is never a casual patron. Drinks she serves match their owner: Drew's martini carries exactly three olives on one pick; Mango's old fashioned is a short rocks glass with one large cube.
