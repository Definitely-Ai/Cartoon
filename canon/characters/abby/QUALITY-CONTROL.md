# Abby Production Quality Control

An output is approved only when every critical gate passes **against the actual generated pixels** — reading the prompt back is not inspection. A good joke or attractive rendering never excuses character drift. Any failed gate is a redraw with the fault named explicitly in the prompt, never a filing. This checklist merges the former rejection checklist and final continuity gate; run it together with `canon/creation/SCENE-QC.md`.

## Critical Identity Gates — All Must Pass

- [ ] Same adult female Westie identity as `full-body-sheet.png`: canine skull, compact head, upright triangular ears, short softly squared muzzle, solid black canine nose.
- [ ] Directional layered white facial fur is present — no smooth generic face, beard, mane, hairstyle, or uniform halo; no human muzzle, nose, ears, or skull.
- [ ] Living medium human-style eyes: visible sclera, separate gray irises, separate black pupils, catchlights, lids, lashes, brow-fur, and aligned intelligent gaze.
- [ ] Eyes are not beads, dots, giant anime eyes, photoreal human implants, empty discs, crossed, or mismatched.
- [ ] Adult feminine hourglass build: fuller bust, narrow waist, slim hips — never childlike, anatomically extreme, explicit, vacant, or impractical.
- [ ] Legs (when a legitimate full-figure scene shows them) are smooth shapely short white fur reading as a clean paper-white contour — not shaggy, muscular, human-skin, paw-ended, bare-footed, or stockinged — with the natural neutral thigh gap.
- [ ] Visible hands have four fingers and one readable thumb — not missing digits, duplicated, fused, paw-like, or disconnected from a prop.
- [ ] Absolutely no tail, tail opening, tail bulge, tuft, or tail-like shape; the rear silhouette and centered back bow are continuous.
- [ ] One close pearl strand with one centered oval faceted gemstone — not missing, a plain choker, a chain, multiplied, or off-center.
- [ ] Blouse is fitted and light with only the top button open over the modest lace inset — not fully buttoned, overly open, missing lace, or replaced.
- [ ] Skirt/apron is very short, dark, and fitted with its centered rear bow — not long, loose, trousers, or implying a tail opening.
- [ ] One folded towel (left shoulder by default, or in use for the work action — never two), bracelet, and black closed-toe mid-height heels are present when visible.
- [ ] No anatomy, clothing, prop, or expression trait has migrated from Drew, Mango, or another character.

Any failure above means **reject and redraw**.

## Scene Physics Gates — All Must Pass

Consistent with `canon/creation/SCENE-QC.md`; check the pixels, not the prompt.

- [ ] **Side of the bar.** Abby is on the service side behind the bar — or in one named owner action (opening/closing, greeting at the swinging doors, checking the room, carrying stock, solving a practical problem). Never a casual patron; no patron on the service side.
- [ ] **Occlusion is correct.** Behind the bar, the counter hides Abby from the upper hip down — that is right, not a fault. Her lower body is never rendered through or in front of the counter, and she is never moved patron-side just to show the outfit.
- [ ] **Counter geometry, both sides.** The counter top hits standing Abby at the upper hip AND seated patrons at forearm height; the floor behind the bar is level with the room.
- [ ] **Seating is real.** Every seated character sits squarely on a stool or seat — hips on the seat, weight believable, legs resolved. Empty foreground stools do not count as someone's seat.
- [ ] **Drinks obey gravity.** Every glass and bottle rests flat on the counter, a coaster, or a shelf, or sits in a real closed grip; patrons' glasses rest ON the counter. Served drinks match their owner: Drew's martini with exactly three olives on one pick; Mango's old fashioned, short rocks glass, one large cube.
- [ ] **Contact points are plausible.** Fingers actually wrap what they hold with the right digit count; towels pass around — not through — vessels; no mitten blobs or pass-through grips.
- [ ] **No interpenetration.** No limb, prop, or garment merges, clips, or passes through the bar, a stool, a glass, or another character.
- [ ] **Scale holds.** Abby keeps the same proportions relative to the counter, stools, doors, bottles, glassware, Drew, and Mango — never resized to match a guest.

## Performance Checks

- [ ] Expression is the default welcoming, intelligent smile — or another approved library expression named by the scene.
- [ ] Emotion reads through lids, brow-fur, gaze, cheek lift, and restrained muzzle shape on the unchanged skull.
- [ ] Gaze targets are stated and readable; in a two-character exchange each character's gaze target is unmistakable.
- [ ] Pose is economical and poised — one clear focal action; no theatrical mugging, giant gestures, slapstick imbalance, airborne poses, or vacant glamour posing.
- [ ] Abby is poised owner-bartender: never cruel, ditzy, drunk, submissive, incompetent, or a casual patron.

## Wardrobe and Scene-Variable Checks

- [ ] The work outfit is worn unless [SCENE] explicitly required different garments; body, collar, and no-tail rule are unchanged under any garment.
- [ ] A previous scene's clothing, prop, damage, or expression has not leaked into this scene.
- [ ] Props are requested or narratively necessary, correctly scaled, and feasibly gripped.
- [ ] Other characters use their own references and preserve their own scale.

## Style Checks

- [ ] Strictly black-and-white: exactly three values (paper white, one mid-gray wash, solid black ink) on warm off-white paper.
- [ ] Linework looks hand-drawn with confident natural variation; fur, lace, and pearl detail clarify rather than clutter.
- [ ] Eyes, nose, collar gemstone, and the essential gag prop carry the strongest useful contrast; background one step lighter.
- [ ] No color, glossy 3D, anime, photorealism, flat vector art, clip-art mascot treatment, dense cross-hatching, or muddy wash.
- [ ] No watermark, signature, speech balloon, or lettering anywhere except the reversed window sign, the short chalkboard lines, and the TV screen.

## Composition and Dialogue Checks

- [ ] Silhouette and focal action read within two seconds at thumbnail size.
- [ ] The crop preserves the anatomy or gesture required for the gag; if scale blurs a locked feature, the rendering is simplified while preserving the rule — never substituted.
- [ ] Clean negative space exists for deterministic dialogue; the image model did not render the caption.
- [ ] The house typesets the exact caption afterward — `file_cartoon` over the studio connector when working there.
- [ ] Panel obeys `canon/comedy/COMEDY-BIBLE.md` and `canon/creation/WORKFLOW.md`.

## Drift Comparison

Before approval, compare the output side by side with `full-body-sheet.png` at the same approximate head size. Do not judge from memory alone.

1. Trace the head, ear, and muzzle silhouette mentally.
2. Compare eye size, placement, and the sclera-iris-pupil-catchlight structure.
3. Compare facial-fur direction and density.
4. Compare bust-waist-hip proportion and total height in her own head units.
5. Compare hand construction and digit count.
6. Compare leg contour, heels, and (in rear views) the continuous no-tail bow silhouette.
7. Compare collar, lace, towel, and bracelet placement.
8. Hide the collar, props, and caption; ask whether the identity still holds.

If a candidate fails twice on the same gate, change the composition rather than rerolling.

Approval threshold: **90/100**, with no critical failure and no category below its minimum.

## Review Record

For every approved or instructive rejected image, record:

- output filename;
- date and reviewer;
- prompt or prompt commit;
- attached reference filenames (master + identity + the one specialist sheet);
- requested expression, work action, wardrobe, and scene;
- total and category scores;
- identity or physics failures and exceptions;
- correction required before reuse.

Rejected images may remain in a dated review folder, but must never be promoted as an identity reference.
