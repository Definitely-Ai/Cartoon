# Drew — Character Bible

**Definitive study:** `canon/vision/drew-plate1-bar-reference.jpg` — the bar
Drew of plate 1. **Authority:** this bible and `canon/HARRINGTON-VISION.md` govern
together; where any older sheet, prompt, or note disagrees, the plates win.
Everything below is written from the founder's own corrections, each one
quoted where it applies.

---

## 0. How to read the numbers on this page

This page has twice carried a confident number that turned out to be wrong when
someone finally measured — *"the brightest object in the panel"* (he is not;
§1), and *"the black is a marking, not a part"* (along the outer third the black
**is** the outline; §3). Both survived because neither said what had been
measured, on which image, between which two points — so neither could be checked
without starting the work from scratch, and nobody did.

So the house rule for every falsifiable claim below:

> **Name the image, name the two endpoints, name the threshold.** A number
> without its landmarks is not a measurement, it is an opinion with a decimal
> point. If you cannot restate a claim that way, it does not belong on this
> page — say the plates do not settle it instead.

Pixel coordinates are `(x, y)` from the top-left of the named file at its native
size. Tone is 8-bit greyscale, 0 = black, 255 = white, quoted as the mean of a
named rectangle. "Dark", where a threshold is needed, means grey **< 110**
unless another figure is given.

Sections **11** and **12** hold what is superseded and what is still open. Read
them before you send a panel back.

## 1. Who he is

Drew is a successful gentleman in his mid-forties who happens to be a
flamingo. He has money, time, and the particular calm of a man who has seen
this cycle before. He is the strip's **arch observer**: the one who names the
mechanism behind the headline in a single clean sentence, then goes back to
his martini.

He is not a mascot, not a muppet, not a caricature of a rich man. He is a
bird at a good bar, and the comedy is entirely in his composure.

| Field | Canon |
| --- | --- |
| Species | Flamingo — a real one, not a humanoid with a beak |
| Age | Mid-forties |
| Role | The dry verdict; the arch observer |
| Default expression | Heavy-lidded, amiable, unimpressed |
| Permanent | Black silk bow tie |
| Drink | Martini — conical stemmed glass, **one olive on one pick** |
| Frame position | **Frame-left. Not "by default" — measured: all eight plate Drews and all twelve filed panels put him frame-left, with Mango frame-right** |
| Value note | Pale, but hatched — **not** the white of the paper, and **not** the brightest thing in the panel |

## 2. The single most important rule

> **He is far more flamingo than human.**
> *(founder: "he's humanoid but a flamingo so he needs feathers — he is more
> flamingo than human; the only thing that's slightly human are his hands.")*

Read that as a ratio, not a style note. Ninety percent bird, ten percent
gentleman:

- **Feathered everywhere.** Breast, shoulders, back, arms — white plumage in
  rows of fine dash-strokes, unbroken **from shoulder to fingertip**.
- **No human skin anywhere.** No bare forearms, no smooth pink limbs, no
  human torso or musculature under the clothes, no human face.

### He is not wearing his feathers

*(founder: "the right arm looks like a jacket — none of the models should look
like they are wearing their fur or feathers.")*

The plumage **grows out of him**. It is never drawn as a garment, and the tell
is always an EDGE where no edge belongs: a cuff, a hem, a seam, a shoulder line,
a lapel, a closing line down the arm.

> **The armhole of his sweater vest is the only edge anywhere on his arm.**
> Below it the plumage runs unbroken and edgeless to his fingertips.
> A second line crossing that arm is a sleeve, and the panel is wrong.

**The fault has a filed example.** `canon/vision/studies/drew.png` is the model
sheet that drew the founder's note. Its frame-left arm is right — ribbed armhole,
then one unbroken feathered surface to the hand. Its frame-right arm is the
failure: a closing line runs from the shoulder down the outside of the arm and
ends in a feather cuff at the wrist, and the arm reads as a knitted sleeve.
**One arm right, one arm wrong, in the same drawing** — which is why the rule is
written as an edge count and not as a feeling.

The same law governs the whole cast: Mango's coat and Abby's are fur under
cloth, meeting the garment at the garment's own edge and nowhere else.

- **He wears no sleeve.** In all eight plate Drews the garment above the waist
  is sleeveless — a V-neck vest, or bare plumage — and the arm is one unbroken
  feathered surface from shoulder to hand. No cloth cuff, no hem, no seam
  anywhere on the arm; the only cuff is the fluff of feathers at the wrist.
  Plate 1's bar Drew is the cleanest reading of it: the ribbed armhole crosses
  the shoulder and nothing crosses the arm below it.
- The **only** humanlike thing about him is the pair of hands at the ends of
  those feathered arms — with one recorded exception in the plates, in §4.

If a panel shows a person-shaped body wearing a flamingo head, it has failed
this bible completely, whatever else is right.

## 3. Construction

### The neck — his signature

A true flamingo neck: **long, high, and swept in a deep, graceful S-curve**,
thick at the shoulders and tapering upward. The founder's correction was
explicit — *"neck curve needs to look more like a flamingo"* — and the bar
plate is the measure. It leaves the shoulders thick, rises in one long sweep,
and turns over at the top so the head hangs forward of where the neck left the
body. **Count the reversals: one.**

### The head

**Small and refined** in proportion to that long neck. The rejected version put
an oversized head on a short neck, and the whole bird changed species.

### The bill

**Take the shape from a swan.** Slender, of even taper, bending in one gentle
unbroken curve, with a dark outer end. That single sentence is what finally
fixed this after six attempts; everything below is detail on it.

**Depth is the rule that matters.** Where the bill meets the feathers, its
depth top-to-bottom is about **one third of its length**, tapering from there
to the tip.

**Measure depth ACROSS the bill, the short way.** Take the line through the
centre of the nostril and measure from the bill's top outline square across to
the lower outline — not straight down the page. The bill is drawn on a steep
slant, so a ruler held vertical reads far too deep. Measure length from the
**centre of the eye** to the **tip**. Both endpoints must land on the drawn
outline: a point that floats inside the pale, or beside the bird, settles
nothing.

On those landmarks the plates were recorded at **0.36** for plate 1's bar Drew
(73 px across, 200 px long), **0.37** for plate 2's lower Drew, and **0.36** and
**0.37** for plate 4's two bar Drews. The renders that drew the founder's
complaint ran **0.64**, nearly twice as deep. *Length was never the fault:* four
rounds were spent shortening a bill that was never too long.

The **200 px** half of that re-measures cleanly: the tile's eye centre is
(187, 64.5) — centroid of the dark eye mass at threshold < 110, x 171–203,
y 55–74 — and eye-centre-to-tip runs **197–200 px** depending on where the tip
is put. **The 73 px half does not re-measure**, and that is an open item; see
§12. Draw to a third; do not send a panel back on the decimal alone.

#### The black carries the outline

This paragraph used to say the opposite — that the black was a marking painted
onto a pale bill, adding nothing to the silhouette. No reference in the
repository supports that. It was an invention, and it lost to the picture on
every single generation.

**Plate 1's security Drew is the clean measurement**, because he is drawn
against a pale wall and every edge is unambiguous. In
`plate-1-security-and-martini-menu.jpg`:

| Landmark | Coordinate | How it was found |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eye centre | **(475, 385)** | Centroid of the dark eye mass, threshold < 110 (x 458–492, y 374–396; 35 × 23 px) |
| Pale/black junction, top edge | **(588, 452)** | Where the bill's upper outline meets the black |
| Pale/black junction, ventral edge | **(537, 512)** | Last row in which the pale bill's lower outline is still pale |
| Bill tip | **(545, 570)** | Last row carrying a dark run inside the bill (y 568, x 538–552); by y 572 it is gone |

From those: eye-centre-to-tip **198 px**; eye-centre-to-junction-midpoint
**131 px**. **The black is the outer 34 percent — the outer third.**

> **The landmark pair decides the answer, so state it.** Measured instead from
> the feather junction at the bill's base (501, 422), the same black is the
> outer **44 percent**. Both numbers are correct; they are answers to different
> questions. *"The outer third"* is true of **eye-centre-to-tip** and of nothing
> else, and that is the pair this page uses.

On the definitive tile the same construction reads: bill base at the feather
junction **(198, 133)**, the pale ventral outline running out at **(263, 177)**
— the last pale run in that row is x 258–267 — and the black carrying the
silhouette on from there. The tile's black **tip cannot be fixed better than
about ±8 px** because it sits against the dark back bar; the best read is
**(288, 234)**. Cut the tip measurement from the security Drew, not from the
tile.

**One bright highlight ribbon runs inside the black, along its length.** On the
security Drew it is a single unbroken pale break through the black — dark runs
per row at threshold < 100 read (565,579) and (584,599) at y 480; (539,564) and
(566,592) at y 520; (541,556) and (561,581) at y 540 — one gap, tracking down
the black from about y 470 to y 545. It is correct, it is drawn on every plate
Drew whose bill is large enough to resolve it, and it was briefly, wrongly,
forbidden.

So the rule, matching the founder's hand:

- **The black is the outer third of the bill's length, measured eye-centre to
  tip**, and along that third it *is* the outline. The silhouette runs
  continuously from pale into black with no step and no notch.
- **It continues the bill's own curve** to the tip. It does not steepen and it
  does not reach past the front of his chest.
- **One bright highlight ribbon runs inside the black**, along its length.
- **The rear two thirds are pale**, top edge and bottom edge alike.
- **Depth stays near a third measured across the bill** — that is the rule doing
  the real work, and it is the one the failing renders broke at 0.64.

> **One deliberate divergence from the source, recorded so nobody "fixes" it
> back.** Most of the plate Drews' black **curls** back and down at the tip:
> plate 4's two bar Drews most sharply — a scythe — and plate 2's lower Drew and
> plate 1's bar Drew clearly. Plate 1's security Drew is the smoothest of the
> eight. **This page forbids the curl anyway**, because every render that was
> given a curled reference amplified it into a talon. If a plate and this rule
> disagree about the curl, the rule wins; that is the only place on this page
> where it does.

*What actually went wrong* in the renders that drew the founder's complaint was
never the construction. It was **scale and curl**: the black ran far longer than
a third, sat on a bill twice as deep as it should be, and hooked back toward the
throat. The plates do the same thing, small and smooth.

**The lesson is the expensive one.** For six rounds the words asked for a bill no
reference showed, while `lib/generate.ts` pinned the label *"the black confined
to a blunt rounded cap"* onto a tile showing the exact opposite. The picture won
every time, as it always will. **Two rounds of rewording with no change is the
signal to stop writing and go look at the picture** — and if the picture and the
words disagree, one of them is wrong, and it is usually not the picture.
`lib/generate.ts` now carries the corrected label; `canon/MASTER-PROMPT.md` does
not. See §10.

Upper and lower halves stay pressed together in one smooth closed line. Pale,
with a fine nostril slit and a small smile-line at its base.

#### The nostril

**One plain thin slit.** No lid, no lash, no catchlight. Given any of those it
renders as a *second eye on the bill*, which has happened.

**Count the lines that close round it: none.** One open arc rides above it and
nothing is drawn round the back.

Measured on the tile at one threshold, so the two are comparable:

| Mark | Extent at threshold < 110 | Size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nostril slit | x 245–261, y 98–106 | **17 × 9 px** |
| Eye | x 171–203, y 55–74 | **33 × 20 px** |

**The slit is about half the eye's length and under half its depth.** An earlier
draft of this page called it *"about 30 × 8 ... as long as the eye"*; on the same
threshold that reads the eye at 33 × 20 the slit reads 17 × 9, so the slit is
half the eye, not its equal. Correcting it matters, because the failure mode is
a nostril that has grown to eye size.

**Half the filed panels ring it.** `sc09-the-total-aloud.png` is the worst: the
nostril is drawn as a **closed lens outline**, a complete ring spanning about
**45 × 40 px** with a filled slit of **27 × 18** inside it, against an eye whose
dark mass is **46 × 41 px**. Same size, same depth, same construction as the eye,
four centimetres in front of it. **One slit, one open arc above it, no closed
outline.**

#### Above the eye

**One fine contour arc.** A single drawn line, the same weight as the smile-line
at the bill's base, curving over the lid and dying away into the crown; between
that line and the crown's dash-strokes the plumage is smooth and pale. The lid
is heavy, the cheek full and softly modelled.

The plates also carry a soft orbital curve **below and behind** the eye — that is
part of the socket and is not the thing being counted. What is being counted is
lines **above** the lid, and the count is one.

#### The two-second test

> Trace the bill's outline with a finger, from the feather line to the tip.
> **Does your finger stay on one line the whole way?**
>
> **PASS** — one continuous outline. Along the rear two thirds it runs over
> pale; along the outer third it runs over black; and **at the handover there is
> no step, no notch and no change of direction.** The black finishes the shape
> the pale started.
> **FAIL** — the pale outline stops and a **deeper** shape starts there: a
> second mass hung off the end, wider than the bill it grew from, or a white
> blade cut through it that is not the one lit ribbon.

*(This test used to say the opposite — that the pale bill's own curve must still
be the edge all the way to the tip. On the outer third it is not, in any of the
eight plates, and as written the old test failed the definitive study. It has
been rewritten to describe the drawing.)*

#### What failed, and why it is worth knowing

*(founder: "fix Drew's beak.")* Six levers were pulled at this one detail. The
log is here because the lessons are not about beaks.

| Lever | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Ratio: "shorter than the head is wide" | **Failed.** Four inspectors, four endpoints for *head width*, ratios from 0.4 to 1.6 **on the same tile** |
| Ratio: "back of skull to bill tip ≤ 1.5×" | **Failed.** On an S-curve neck **there is no back of the skull** — the head merges into the neck and the landmark cannot be found |
| New reference tile (plate 4) | **Necessary, not sufficient.** Closed the gape; the renders copied its hook |
| Model effort raised to medium | **No change to the shape.** Finer linework, same bill |
| A second reference tile, close on the head | **No change** — while its label still described a bill the tile did not show. It is now shipped with a corrected label (§10) |
| "The black covers the front third" | **Partial win** — fixed the black's distribution where the relational rule had not |
| **Positive referent: "take the shape from a swan"** | **Fixed it** |

Three laws came out of it, and they apply to every rule in every bible here:

1. **Countable beats relational.** *"The front third"* changed the drawing;
   *"level with the chin"* did not. A relation the drawing has to compute is a
   rule it can ignore.
2. **Negation summons.** The rule forbidding a wild flamingo's bill *named a
   wild flamingo's bill* — and produced one for three rounds. These models read
   co-occurrence, not negation. Name the right thing instead.
3. **The reference out-votes the text.** When three rounds of rewording move
   nothing, stop rewording and go look at the conditioning image.

#### A standing warning about the source

Of the eight Drews in the four plates:

- **Plate 3's is the only one with the bill gaped open.** It was the identity
  tile for three rounds. Never cut from it.
- **Plate 4's bar Drews end in a curved black hook**, which the renders amplified
  into a talon. Plate 4's golf Drew *does* wear the full wardrobe, vest included
  — the older note here said otherwise and was wrong. He also wears a soft
  closed-crown cap with a short brim rather than his visor. **The visor is the
  house choice**, kept so his small refined skull stays visible and so his golf
  headgear never reads as Mango's ball cap.
- **Plate 1 holds two Drews, and the tile is the lower one.** Cut from the bar
  panel only. The security Drew above him is not a cutting hazard for the black
  — his construction is the cleanest in the set and §3's measurements come from
  him — but he is in an airport, with human beings in shot, and a tile cut from
  him would teach the room wrong.
- **The bar Drew is the tile because he is the closest wardrobe-complete Drew at
  usable resolution — not because his bill is perfect.** His black is blunter and
  rounder than plate 4's scythe, but it curls, and it carries the highlight
  ribbon. This sheet forbids the curl and the tile teaches it anyway.
- **The full wardrobe — shirt collar, bow tie, V-neck vest — appears on three of
  the eight:** plate 1's security Drew, plate 1's bar Drew, plate 4's golf Drew.
  Four more wear a plain white **collar band** and bow tie over the bare
  feathered breast — plate 2's two and plate 4's two bar Drews. Plate 3's wears
  the bow tie knotted straight onto the neck feathers, with no collar at all.
  **We dress him fully in every panel regardless: that is a house rule, set in
  `canon/MASTER-PROMPT.md`, not a majority of the plates, and it is not up for a
  recount.** All twelve filed panels wear the vest, so nothing in current output
  shows what his shoulders are built like.
- **No bill-length ranking is recorded here.** Every head-normalised ratio tried
  on these plates has failed the same way — see the table above: four inspectors,
  four endpoints, on one tile — and plate 3's bill is gaped, so it has no single
  length. Before such a number goes in this block, name the two landmarks it was
  measured between.

**And cut it at native resolution.** The tile that preceded the current one,
`canon/vision/drew-bar-reference.jpg`, is a 2.94× upscale of a 300-pixel region
of plate 4: mean pixel gradient **3.40**. The model was handed a smear whose most
salient dark shape was the hooked tip.

The figure is `mean(|dI/dx|) + mean(|dI/dy|)`, halved, over the greyscale. Write
the formula down whenever the number is quoted or it cannot be checked. Recomputed
for this revision:

| Image | Gradient |
| --- | --- |
| `plate-1-security-and-martini-menu.jpg` | **7.10** |
| `plate-2-debt-ceiling-and-retirement.jpg` | **4.78** |
| `plate-3-national-mall.jpg` | **5.84** |

The plates span 4.78–8.35, not the 7–8 an older note claimed. **A tile is sharp
enough when it is at or above its own plate**; 7.91 and 8.49 against plate 1's
7.10 both clear that.

### The eyes

**Heavy-lidded and deadpan**: visible white, a distinct dark iris and pupil, and
an upper lid that comes down across the eye as one long low line. They read as
dry, unimpressed intelligence. Never a flat bird-dot.

The lid is the whole effect, and the plates carry it with **one line above it,
not two**: a single lid margin across the eye, one fine contour arc above that,
and between the arc and the crown's dash-strokes **smooth pale plumage**. On the
tile that smooth zone runs from the arc at about y 45 up to the first crown
dashes at about y 28 — roughly **15–20 px** deep above an eye whose dark mass is
**33 px** wide. Dashes arced over the lid read as lashes or a ridge; `sc09` draws
them, and it is the most severe head in the batch.

**The eye's proportions are not the place to send a panel back.** An earlier
draft recorded the opening's depth over width as **0.50** in both plates and
panels, and the iris at **0.76** of the way from the rear corner to the front in
the plates against **0.73** in the panels. Those numbers never named their
endpoints, and they do not reproduce: measuring the **dark mass** at threshold
< 110 gives 33 × 20 (ratio 0.61) on plate 1's bar Drew and 35 × 23 (ratio 0.66)
on plate 1's security Drew. The two readings are measuring different things —
one the drawn opening, one the ink. **Do not reject an eye on this number until
the two endpoints are written down here.** Reject it on the thing you can see:
a bird-dot, a shelf, dashes over the lid, or a nostril grown to eye size.

### The face — amiable, never a ghoul

> *(founder: "Drew looks scary.")*

The deadpan is **fond**. Plump, softly modelled cheeks with fine stipple; a faint
smile-line; the look of a man who has heard the story before and likes you anyway.
**Never** gaunt, hollow-eyed, sunken, skull-like, staring, or sinister. He is
a gentleman at his club.

### The body

Plump and soft-bodied — a well-fed bird, not an athletic one. Rounded breast,
soft shoulders.

## 4. Hands — the one human thing

At the end of each feathered arm is a **genuine hand**: **feather-covered**,
with a soft fluffy cuff at the wrist, and long scalloped tiers of wing feathers
hanging beneath each forearm.

**How much of the hand separates depends on the view.**

| Drew | What the plate draws |
| --- | --- |
| Plate 1, security | Four fingers with drawn knuckle creases and a working thumb, flat on the tray; the other hand holds a card in a full grip |
| Plate 1, bar | Four digits closed on the stem, with dark nail crescents on the top two |
| Plate 2, both | Two to three soft lobes folded at the bowl's base, the mitt crossing in front of the stem |
| Plate 3 | Three fingertips clear of the stem, thumb behind — the most legible grip in the set |
| Plate 4, two bar Drews | Three soft lobes closing on the stem |
| Plate 4, golf, drink hand | Three soft lobes closing on the stem |
| Plate 4, golf, club hand | **No hand at all** — see below |

**Three digits closed on a stem is the floor, not the target** — and a fused
paddle with no division in it is only on model at plate 4's distance from camera,
never in a close bar panel.

**The recorded exception, and it is not a licence.** Plate 4's golf Drew rests
his frame-left limb on a golf club, and that limb ends in a **wingtip with three
long black-tipped primaries** — no fingers, no thumb, no grip. It is the only
limb in the eight plates that has no hand on it. It is recorded here because
this page must not claim the plates are unanimous when they are not. **It is
still not what we draw.** The founder's standing order overrides it, and a bar
panel that ends an arm in black claws is a redraw.

What is required in every case is that the grip be real: **a digit crosses in
front of the stem**, the digits do not pass through it, and the glass is
genuinely supported.

The standing order for the whole cast:

> *"Focus on the hands — they need to have feathers and fur but be humanoid."*

Texture stays animal; construction stays human. Every finger distinctly drawn —
**four fingers and an opposed thumb**, the count `canon/HARRINGTON-VISION.md`
sets from the founder's note *"his hands need to be changed … more human."*

**The martini is the test.** His hand must look natural and graceful holding
the glass **by the stem**, in a dainty, pinky-elegant grip. If the hand looks
awkward, clubbed, or like a wing pretending to be a hand, redraw it. This is
the single most-looked-at detail in the strip.

## 5. Wardrobe

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| **Bow tie** | Small, solid black, silk, sitting at the throat. **His default skin** — never without it. **The bow is the only black anywhere at his neck:** no black band, ribbon, strap, cord or choker runs around the neck or passes behind the bow. *(The white shirt collar does circle the neck — that is the collar, and it is right. What is banned is black.)* **Drawn as a tied bow, anatomically:** two wings of equal size spreading left and right, each wider at its outer edge, pinched to a small centre knot — horizontal, level, centred under the chin, resting ON the collar rather than floating in front of it. Never one lump, never a knotless butterfly, never tilted or vertical, never more than two wings |
| **Collar** | **A crisp white turned-down shirt collar with points** — a stand that circles the base of the neck, its points spreading at the front, the bow tie's knot resting on it. This is what all three wardrobe-complete plate Drews wear (plate 1 security, plate 1 bar, plate 4 golf). The plain **collar band** with no points, seen on plate 2's two Drews and plate 4's two bar Drews, belongs to the vest-less dress and is not the house look |
| **Shirt** | Pale, collared, with a visible placket; **two buttons** show below the bow tie in every wardrobe-complete plate Drew |
| **Sweater vest** | V-neck, fine knit with distinctly **ribbed edges at the V and at both armholes** — the hem is ribbed too, but no plate and no filed panel gets low enough to show it. Worn over the shirt. On the plates the stitch texture is fine enough to sit close in weight to his plumage, so **the ribbing and the V are the only things making it read as knitwear**: lose them and the vest becomes more plumage |
| **Trousers** | Yes *(founder: "he needs to have pants")* — **and never in frame.** No plate and no filed panel puts anything below his chest in shot; the counter crops him. The studio study `canon/vision/studies/drew.png` does show them — pale, flat-front, one hand in the pocket — but a study is not a plate. This row says what he owns |
| **Golf** | Adds a pale **visor**, worn **in addition to** the bow tie, never instead of it. (Plate 4's golf Drew wears a soft closed-crown cap instead; the visor is the house choice, see §3) |

fishing vest and a tuxedo, all with sleeves. It is superseded; see §11.

## 6. Props and staging

- **The martini** is always within reach at the bar — conical stemmed glass,
  **one olive on one pick**, standing on a square napkin or coaster. Lifted by
  the stem.
- **The olive count, recounted for this revision.** Of the seven Drew martinis
  in the plates, **six hold exactly one olive and the seventh — plate 4's golf
  Drew — holds two.** Of the **twelve** filed panels, **all twelve hold exactly
  one.** An earlier draft of this page said the panels held *"two in seven of ten
  and three in two more"*; that is wrong on the count of panels and wrong on the
  count of olives. Every olive in every plate and every panel is threaded on a
  pick; none floats loose in the cone.
- **One bowl, one stem, one foot, and the foot stands on something.** In every
  plate the glass either stands on its own foot on the marble or is lifted in a
  closed grip with a digit crossing in front of the stem. The cone never ends in
  a hand, a nub, or the bottom edge of the panel.
- **Where the filed panels break it**, re-inspected for this revision:
  - `sc09` — the mitt is closed but sits **alongside** the stem; no digit crosses
    in front of it, and the glass is standing on its own foot rather than being
    held. That is a hand near a glass, not a hand holding one.
  - `sc10` — the grip is real, digits closed on the stem, but the **foot hangs
    below the marble's top edge** with nothing under it.
  - `sc01` is **not** a fault, and an earlier draft said it was: it claimed the
    cone stopped at (255,1425) with no stem, no foot and no counter under it. It
    does not. sc01's glass has bowl, stem and foot standing on a square coaster
    on the marble, and (255,1425) lands on Drew's forearm plumage. That claim was
    written against a version of the batch that is no longer filed — which is the
    real lesson: **a coordinate is only evidence against the file it was measured
    in, so name the file and the date.**
- **The grip still has no reference tile.** `drew-plate1-bar-reference.jpg` is
  380 × 520, and the martini enters it only at the bottom right corner: about
  ninety pixels of bowl rim and one olive — no stem, no foot, no hand. The
  second tile, `drew-plate1-head-study.jpg`, is 335 × 275 and is head only. The
  detail this sheet calls the single most-looked-at in the strip is conditioned
  on words alone. If it drifts again, **recut a tile to include the whole glass
  and the hand. Do not rewrite the sentence.**
- **Frame-left** — in all eight plate Drews and all twelve filed panels — neck
  arched so his face hangs where the gag needs it, eye sliding sideways at Mango.
- **He is a patron, never staff.** We look toward the bar from the dining room,
  so the **back bar with its bottles and mirror is behind him — and that is
  correct.** What settles it is the **marble counter between him and them**: he
  sits on the room side, our side. He is never in the service well, never
  pouring, and **never holding a liquor bottle or a bar towel** — a gentleman
  holding either reads as the bartender, and that was the original fault.
- Bar panels are **cropped at the counter** — chest-up, no legs, no stools.
- **He never looks at us.** His gaze belongs inside the room — at Mango, at the
  television, at the chalkboard, or down at the glass. The reader is watching
  through a fourth wall nobody in the bar knows is there. **Nobody in the panel
  ever looks out at the reader**, and a Drew who has turned his eye down the lens
  is a redraw, however good the head is.

### The house name

- **It is built into the object, and never onto him.** Nothing on Drew is ever
  lettered: not the sweater vest, the shirt, the collar, the visor, and least of
  all the bow tie. The mark lives on **objects** — the golf bag, the napkin under
  the martini, a coaster, a matchbook, a bill folder, an umbrella *(founder: "not
  on Mango or Drew, but brand certain things — and it must look really great,
  like it was part of the object")*.
- **It is made the way the material is made.** Embossed or debossed into the
  leather of the bag and the bill folder; woven into the weave of a napkin;
  pressed into the pulp of a coaster; foil-stamped on a matchbook; burned into
  the grain of a tray; etched into glass. It follows the surface's curve, takes
  the same light and shadow as the material around it, and anything passing in
  front of it breaks it. One short line in the house script, small and neat,
  sized to the object. **If removing the lettering would leave the object looking
  untouched, it was printed on top and it is wrong.** Never a sticker, decal,
  patch, badge or floating label.
- **It rides on portable things only**, and it is *additional* to whatever
  lettering the brief already commissions. The full rule, with the four tests,
  is `canon/settings/SETTINGS-BIBLE.md` § *The house name is built into the
  object*; this section is Drew's slice of it and does not diverge from it.
- **No plate demonstrates it yet.** The plates carry the house name on wall
  signs, on the mirrored front window and on the 19th-hole course sign — all
  fixtures. Plate 4's golf bag carries only Mango's *FORE! NOT SO FAST* towel;
  the leather itself is unmarked. Plate 1's, plate 2's and plate 4's coasters are
  blank. **The made-into-the-material mark is a house rule from the founder's
  note, not something the plates show.** Say so when you brief it, and do not
  expect the model to copy it out of a reference.

## 7. Acting

Drew's register is **understatement**. He reclassifies things: he takes a
headline and renames it into what it actually is. He does not shout, explain
the joke, or panic — and he never cusses or slanders.

His lines are verdicts:

- *"Rates will come down. I've simply stopped asking when."*
- *"Committed is money that hasn't had its second thoughts yet."*
- *"The target follows the price at a respectful distance. It's called research."*

Against Mango's worry he is the still point. Against Abby's authority he defers
with amusement — she settles the argument, and he enjoys that.

### What his face does

Everything above is about the caption. This part is about the drawing, and it is
here because the whole filed batch came back with one head. **His bill never
opens, so it carries no expression at all.** The performance is the lid, the
iris, and the angle of his neck.

The neck angle is the one that is measurably stuck. Take the line from the centre
of his eye to the black bill tip, and read its angle below horizontal. Across the
plates it runs from about **40°** — head carried up, plate 4's globe Drew — to
about **56°**, head bent over the drink, plate 2's lower Drew. Ten of the filed
panels were measured at **56° ± 4°**, nine of them inside a single 7-degree band.

**The measurement needs its second endpoint pinned down before it is used to
reject anything.** Re-measured on `sc01`: the eye's dark mass centres at
(374, 636); the black of the bill runs to an extreme tip near (459, 847) and has
its centroid near (494, 790). Eye-to-centroid reads **52°**; eye-to-extreme-tip
reads **68°**. Both are "the angle", and they are sixteen degrees apart. Until
§12 item 4 is closed, quote the endpoint with the number.

What is not in doubt, because it is visible at a glance on a contact sheet of the
batch: **not one of the twelve filed panels carries the head up.** That is one
head, drawn twelve times.

**In any batch, at least one panel in four carries the head up at 40–46°** — three
in a batch of twelve, minimum — and when the line is Drew's, draw him with the
head up. The house types a name in front of the caption; the reader should not
need it.

## 8. Forbidden — any one of these is a redraw

- A human body, human skin, bare forearms, or a human face
- A neck with no reversal in it, or one short enough to sit the head on the shoulders
- A gaped bill; a bill deeper than **0.45** measured across at the nostril line over its eye-centre-to-tip length (the plates were recorded at 0.36–0.37 — draw to a third)
- An oversized head
- More than one line above the eye — a shelf, a second raised lobe, or feather dashes arced over the lid
- A nostril with a closed outline round it, or with a lid, lash or catchlight; a nostril grown to the size of the eye
- **A second dark mass hung off the end of the bill:** the pale outline stops and a *deeper* shape starts there, wider than the bill it grew from, with a step or a notch at the handover. *(The black itself carrying the outline along the outer third is correct — §3. So is the one bright highlight ribbon inside it. A second white blade, or a white blade that breaks the silhouette, is not.)*
- The black curling back underneath toward his throat, or reaching past the front of his chest
- A gaunt, hollow-eyed, skull-like or sinister look
- Missing bow tie; any black band, strap, ribbon or cord around the neck behind the bow
- A bow tie without a centre knot, with one wing or more than two, tilted off horizontal, or floating in front of the collar instead of resting on it
- Any cuff, hem or seam crossing the arm — plumage drawn as a sleeve
- Wingtips or claws instead of hands, or hands that can't hold a stem
- Fewer than three digits closed on the stem, or no digit crossing in front of it
- A martini drawn as a bowl alone — no stem, no foot, nothing underneath it
- More than one olive, or an olive floating free of the pick
- Standing behind the bar among the bottles; a liquor bottle or bar towel in his hands
- Eyes turned out of the panel at the reader
- Lettering anywhere on Drew himself — vest, shirt, collar, visor or bow tie
- A house mark that sits ON a surface rather than IN it: a decal, sticker, patch or flat label that ignores the material, the curve and the light

## 9. Approval standard

A panel passes when a reader who knows the plates would say *that is the same
bird* — and when the panel would look right hanging beside them. Long neck,
small head, amiable eye, bow tie, feathers to the fingertips, and a martini
held like a gentleman.

## 12. Open — not settled by any plate

Honesty about an unknown is worth more than a confident guess. These are the
things this revision could not close.

1. **The bill's depth measurement does not reproduce.** The ratio 0.36 is
   recorded with a length (eye centre to tip, ~200 px, which does check out) and
   a depth (73 px across at the nostril line, which does not). Scanning
   perpendicular to the bill's axis on the tile returns anything from 41 to 89 px
   depending on where the scan starts, because the mouth line inside the bill and
   the dark back bar outside it both stop the scan. **This is the same "four
   inspectors, four endpoints" failure the §3 table logs.** Before the number is
   used to reject a panel, someone must write down the two coordinates the 73 px
   was measured between.
2. **The tile's black bill tip is not locatable to better than ±8 px.** It sits
   against a dark back bar. Every tip-dependent number on this page is therefore
   quoted from plate 1's **security** Drew, who is drawn against a pale wall.
3. **The eye ratios (0.50 depth-over-width; iris at 0.76 / 0.73) have no
   landmarks and do not reproduce.** See §3. Do not send a panel back on them.
4. **The neck angle has an undefined endpoint, and was measured on ten panels
   when the batch is twelve.** *"The centroid of the black bill tip"* is not a
   point anyone can find twice: on `sc01` the centroid of the whole black reads
   52° and its extreme tip reads 68°. The figure `56° ± 4°` and the "nine inside
   a 7-degree band" belong to a measurement made when this page believed the
   batch was ten; which two panels were never measured is not recorded.
   **Define the second endpoint — extreme tip, and say so — then re-measure all
   twelve and write the eye and tip coordinates down beside each angle.**
5. **The made-into-the-material house mark has no example anywhere in the
   plates** (§6). It is a founder's instruction we have not yet seen drawn.
6. **Nothing in current output shows his shoulders.** All twelve filed panels
   wear the vest, and every plate crops at a counter or a tray. What the
   feathered shoulder looks like under the armhole is undrawn.
7. **The clawed wingtip on plate 4's golf Drew** (§4) is a real disagreement
   between the plates and the house rule, resolved here in favour of the house
   rule. If the founder ever wants the wingtip back, this is the note to bring
   him.
8. **The master prompt still contradicts this page on the bill's black** (§10),
   and this page cannot fix it — the model reads that file, not this one. Until
   someone edits `canon/MASTER-PROMPT.md`, expect the black to keep drifting, and
   do not read a drifting black as a failure of the reference tile.
