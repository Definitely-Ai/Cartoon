# The window — CONSTANT

**Status: CONSTANT. No caption, gag, brief or writer ever touches it.** It is
the same window, showing the same city, in every bar cartoon.

## The locked description (operator ruling, 2026-08-31 — supersedes the plates)

- **ONE LARGE MODERN WINDOW** filling the street wall at frame-left: a single
  tall sheet of clear plate glass. **No small panes, no muntin bars, no
  divided lights, no frosting** — the old multi-pane, half-frosted window in
  the plates is retired.
- **Through it: present-day New York City.** The strip is set TODAY. Glass-
  and-steel towers over older stone facades, a modern street — contemporary,
  unmistakably NYC, drawn in the same engraved hand.
- **Nobody outside.** The street carries architecture and daylight, never
  people (the television screen remains the strip's only place for humans).
- **The one mark it carries:** the bar's name mirrored on the glass as read
  from inside, spelled letter by letter — T-H-E S-W-I-N-G-I-N-G D-O-O-R, both
  G's present. Nothing else is ever lettered on or beyond the glass: no shop
  signs, no billboards, no bus ads readable through the window.
- No door is drawn in it or beside it.

## What a caption may do to it

Nothing. Not weather, not time of day, not a sign outside, not a passer-by.
A gag that needs the window changed is a rewrite.
