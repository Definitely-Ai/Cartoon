Subject: The Swinging Door - end of day, September 8: the bar is built, and a working system by the 15th

Hi Rick,

Today's report is attached. Thirteen hours today, from twenty to ten this morning to ten to eleven tonight, and a hundred and seventy-three pictures made and judged. The short version: the room is finished and it is good, the characters are the one real piece of work left, and there is a dated plan in the report that ends on Monday the fifteenth with the whole thing working.

The good first. The back bar is now cut into the wall instead of hung on it - two rows of bottles in a recess in the panelling with the drinks ledge running underneath, and the wall's own look left alone. It was designed in the morning, drawn once and approved. The bottles were rebuilt properly after that: real shapes at real sizes, filled to the shoulder, with the labels set in type afterwards rather than left to the machine to scribble. And this evening, for the first time, Drew, Barclay and Abby were all in the room together.

Now the part that went wrong. The way we got them in there was wrong. We spent the afternoon drawing each object and each character on its own and pasting them together, and a picture made of pieces never reads as one drawing - the heads belonged to different drawings from the bodies and the light came from three different places. I threw it out. We also lost hours to a plain mistake: when our machine loads a model, the first picture takes ten to twenty minutes and every one after that takes a minute, and we kept reading that first pause as a crash and restarting it. That is understood now and watched for.

Here is why I am not worried about it. Late tonight we stopped assembling and handed the approved room and the three portraits to the same hand that drew the plates you accepted, and let it draw the whole picture in one pass. All three read as themselves, in one pen, with real bottles behind them and no seams. A few passes later both gentlemen were seated in their own chairs on our side of the bar, seen from behind, exactly the staging of your plate. The hard part happened tonight.

So we simplify from here. The bottles get perfected on their own tomorrow and come to you for approval. Then Drew, Barclay and Abby get drawn again from their reference photographs and their written descriptions, because they were designed for a different machine than the one we run now - one character at a time, each one shown and approved before the next begins. The room, the chairs, the window and the sign stay built in code so they never drift; everything that has to look drawn gets drawn in one pass.

The dates: the 9th, the bottles. The 10th and 11th, the cast, one at a time. The 12th, the three of them in the room with the poses a joke needs. The 13th and 14th, the pipeline end to end - a caption goes in, a finished plate comes out, with the daily report and the desk filled in as it goes. The 15th, a complete working system turning out funny cartoons with a real idea underneath and detail worth looking at twice.

The report shows all of it, including the version I rejected and the seed where the two of them swapped clothes. Nothing is dressed up. If a day on that list slips, you will hear it from me on the day it slips, not on the fifteenth.

Talk tomorrow.
