# DREW — LOOK CARD (anchor pose DREW-02)

## HUGGABLE BUT PROFESSIONAL — what it means for Drew

**He is a gentleman at his club, not a ghoul.** Ninety percent bird, ten percent gentleman — but the ten percent is the part you would sit next to. He is plump and soft-bodied, a well-fed bird and not an athletic one: rounded breast, soft shoulders, full softly modelled cheeks with fine stipple and a faint smile-line at the base of the bill. If a render comes back gaunt, hollow-eyed, sunken, skull-like, staring or sinister, it is not Drew, however good the drawing is. The founder's word for that render was "scary"; the bible's answer is "the deadpan is fond."

**THE EYES.** Human, knowing, warm — never a bird-dot, never a bead, never a blank. Built as: visible white, a distinct dark iris and pupil, and one long low upper lid coming down across the eye. The lid IS the effect. Above it exactly ONE fine contour arc, and between that arc and the crown's dash-strokes the plumage stays smooth and pale — two lines above the eye, or dashes arced over the lid, turn him into a scowling man with eyebrows (which is what the published panels do). The eye reads as dry, unimpressed intelligence, a man who has heard the story before and likes you anyway. It always has a target inside the room; a Drew who has turned his eye down the lens is a redraw.

**EXPRESSION RANGE.** His bill never opens, so it carries no expression at all — the performance is the lid, the iris and the angle of the neck. Amused, skeptical, concerned, quietly pleased, listening: all of it is one lid line, one iris position and one head angle. The only bill event in the whole canon is the small parting along the outer half when the line is his (DREW-03). Skeptical without becoming sour, cruel, frantic, foolish or cynical.

**POSTURE.** Seated, weight in the leather chair, shoulders inside its arms, cropped at the counter chest-up. The neck is thick at the shoulders and rises in one long sweep, turning over at the top so the head hangs FORWARD of where the neck left the body — count the reversals: one. Never straight, never goose-stiff, never short. The head angle is the pose variable that matters: eye-centre to bill tip, 40–46 degrees below horizontal is head UP (the anchor, and the angle to use whenever the line is his), 56 degrees is head bent over the drink. Head up in at least one panel in four.

**WARDROBE AT THE BAR.** Crisp white turned-down shirt collar with points; small solid-black silk bow tie, two equal wings pinched to a centre knot, level, resting ON the collar — no black band, strap, cord or ribbon anywhere at the neck. V-neck honeycomb sweater vest, ribbed at the V and at BOTH armholes, sleeveless always, over a pale collared shirt with a visible placket and two buttons below the bow. Trousers he owns but the counter crops them. Conical stemmed martini, ONE olive on ONE pick, standing on a square coaster; lifted by the stem in a dainty pinky-elegant grip. Nothing on him is ever lettered.

## BEAUTIFUL TEXTURE FOR HIS SPECIES — the stroke language the bible locks

The whole panel is an antique steel engraving: fine pen crosshatching and stippling on every surface, confident contour lines over the hatching, full tonal range from rich blacks through silvery greys to bright paper. Drew's share of that pen is three zones:

1. **Head, neck and breast** — white plumage laid in ROWS of fine dash- and crescent-strokes, following the round of the neck, dense enough to model it, opening out into the smooth pale zone above the eye's contour arc.
2. **Forearms and the backs of the hands** — the same fine downy dash-strokes running UNBROKEN across wrist, forearm and hand back, with a soft fluffy cuff at the wrist. Nothing about him is smooth pink or bare human skin anywhere.
3. **Long scalloped tiers of wing feathers hanging beneath each forearm** — larger, layered, individually outlined leaf-shaped plumes with internal barb strokes. No trailing wing-drape past the hip, no display tail.

**The edge count is the texture rule.** The armhole of his sweater vest is the ONLY edge anywhere on his arm; below it the plumage runs unbroken and edgeless to the fingertips. A second line crossing that arm is a sleeve and the panel is wrong. Feathers grow out of him; they are never drawn as a garment.

**Feather against knit.** They are close in weight on the plates, so they must be told apart by DIRECTION and REPEAT: plumage is directional overlapping dashes following the body's round; the vest is a regular honeycomb lattice with hard ribbed borders at the V and the armholes. Lose the ribbing and the vest becomes more plumage.

**Value.** He is pale but HATCHED — high midtone under fine dash-strokes, not bare paper. He is not the brightest object in the panel (Barclay outreads him by 30–40 grey levels) and nothing in the panel is paper white. Strongest contrast goes to the eye, the black of the bill, the bow tie and the martini.

**THE BILL, because it has cost six rounds.** Take the shape from a swan: slender, of even taper, bending in one gentle unbroken curve. Depth across the bill at the feather line is about one third of its eye-centre-to-tip length (plates 0.36–0.37; the renders the founder rejected ran 0.64). The black is the OUTER THIRD of that length and along that third it IS the outline — the silhouette runs continuously from pale into black with no step and no notch, it continues the bill's own curve, it does not steepen, it does not reach past the front of his chest, and ONE bright highlight ribbon runs inside it. The rear two thirds are pale, top edge and bottom edge alike. No curl, no hook back toward the throat — the house rule beats the plates on this one detail. One plain thin nostril slit, about half the eye's length, one open arc above it, nothing drawn round the back.

## THE THREE THINGS PAST RENDERS GOT WRONG

1. **The black on the bill grew into a talon.** Every failing round made it deeper, longer and hooked: a second dark mass hung off the end, a blunt cap over top and bottom, the tip curling back and down toward the throat. Measured, the rejected renders ran 0.64 depth against the plates' 0.36. `cartoons/2026-08-04-an-emerging-asset` and `2026-08-11-the-retirement-number` both draw it as a deep hooked black cap; even the approved plate `canon/plates/duo.png` carries a long downward curl past the mandible (which is why `head.png` ships with the note that the curl is the one place the rule outranks the plate). Fix is a positive referent — a swan — plus the outer-third/outline/highlight-ribbon wording, never another negation.
2. **He got undressed, or he got sleeved.** All six published cartoons drop the wardrobe entirely: bow tie tied onto bare neck feathers, no pointed collar, no shirt, no sweater vest, wing-mitts instead of hands, long bird legs and webbed feet, three olives on the pick. The opposite failure is on the other side of the same rule — `canon/vision/studies/drew.png` runs a closing line down the frame-right arm ending in a feather cuff, so that arm reads as a knitted sleeve, and `canon/vision/example-drew.jpg` gives him a sleeved shirt with buttoned cuffs and bare human hands. One arm right, one arm wrong, in the same drawing: hence the edge count.
3. **One head, drawn twelve times, with the wrong eye in it.** Not one of the twelve filed panels carries the head up; all sit at 56 degrees ± 4, bent over the drink. And the eye in them is not the bible's: the published panels draw a heavy dark human eyebrow ABOVE a second contour line (two lines above the eye), half the filed batch rings the nostril into a closed lens that reads as a second eye four centimetres in front of the first, and where the lid goes flat the eye drops to a bird-dot. Head angle, lid line, one arc, one slit — those four are the whole performance, and they are what to check first on the crop.

# Barclay

BARCLAY — LOOK CARD (anchor: figure-barclay-02-toward)

HUGGABLE BUT PROFESSIONAL, FOR BARCLAY
He is a successful gentleman in his mid-forties who is unmistakably a golden retriever, and the huggable half is not softness of drawing — it is warmth of face. The founder's correction is the whole card: "when we initially look at Mango he looks sad; the more you look the happier he gets. I'd like something to change slightly so he looks happier." The fix is a split the drawing must obey every time: THE WORRY LIVES IN THE BROWS, EVERYTHING BELOW THEM IS WARM. Four marks, in this order — (1) BROWS: both inner ends lifted, one faint crease across the forehead, and that is the whole of the worry; (2) MOUTH: closed, the fine black lip line with the corner hook lifting ABOVE the level of the line's middle — that lifted corner is his entire smile, he never opens his mouth to be amused; (3) EYES: bright and open, the upper lid CLEAR of the dark, one clean catchlight; (4) CHEEKS: full and lifted, the fur under the eye rising toward the ear. A viewer reads a good-humoured man who is a little concerned, never a downcast one.
THE EYES — HUMAN, KNOWING, WARM. Built in three marks and nothing more: one solid dark mass pressed against the FRONT corner of the opening (nearest the muzzle) taking the front 40% and no more; PAPER-WHITE sclera filling the rear 60% — genuinely the paper, measured 203-215 against a cheek of 198; one catchlight punched bright inside the dark. At the size he is drawn a separately drawn grey iris and black pupil do not resolve — do not spend a line on them. What must never merge is the dark and the white; that white is where the gentleman lives, and it is what makes the look knowing rather than animal. The lid rests on the TOP of the dark and no lower — Drew is the low-lidded one. He never looks at the reader; his eyes go to Drew, to Abby, to the television, or down to his glass.
EXPRESSION RANGE (three countable handles — brows, lids, mouth; a different one every panel): braced (hearing a number he does not like) brows up, lids open, mouth closed and firm; amused, lid a little down and the back corner of the closed lip lifted further; paying, chin down, eyes to the glass or the paper, brows still up; and THE ONE PARTED MOUTH, saved for the beat where he is actually saying the line — a speaking mouth, never a resting one. His hands act too: one posture beat per panel and a different one each time — chin on a closed fist, flat palm on the marble, two fingers on the rim of the glass, the glass slid an inch toward Abby. Two panels running with the same face and the same hands is a batch that has stopped acting.
POSTURE. Middle-aged and solid — rounded shoulders, broad soft chest, modest belly, comfortable and not athletic; judge his weight from the shoulders (in a bar panel the belly is below the crop). He is dignified while he says the expensive thing: he is never the butt of the joke, he is its accountant. Seated on the room side of the marble, chest-up crop, back three-quarter with the head turned back and across to Drew, the near hand at the old fashioned. ABSOLUTELY NO TAIL — his back meets the chair and that is the end of him.
WARDROBE AS WORN AT THE BAR. The dark suit jacket over a pale open-collared shirt, NO TIE, no cap indoors (a cap at the bar is a fault). The small USA flag pin is PERMANENT on his LEFT lapel — dark canton with the stars stippled pale, alternating bars, a fine staff along the hoist dropping a little below the flag; judge the pin on the canton, not on whether something is there. The plain round-dialled wristwatch on a dark band rides the same left side: two marks down one side of one man. Nothing else on him is ever lettered, crested or monogrammed. His old fashioned — short rocks glass, one large cube, one dark cherry — stands on its coaster within reach or sits in a real closed five-finger grip; the shared nut bowl stands on the marble between the two of them and is not optional. He never holds a bottle or a bar towel.
